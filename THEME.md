# BETWEEN SECONDS — Theme & World Pitch (v2, time-loop)

Working title. Tagline: *"She dies at 6:14. You have all the time in the world."*

Status: PITCH. **[YOUR CALL]** marks slots left open on purpose.

---

## 1. The Premise

**June** is a physicist. She built the **Anchor Engine** — the first working time machine — and calibrated it the only way physics allowed: by pinging fixed "anchor points" scattered through deep history. The Cretaceous impact layer. A foundation stone at Giza. An eclipse over Delphi. A comet that hung over medieval Europe for forty nights. Her ladder of anchors runs from the age of dinosaurs to her lab.

**[Player — working name: Cole]** is her partner. Not a scientist — a martial artist; he teaches at a dojo across town and understands maybe a tenth of what she does. On the evening of the first live test he shows up at the lab with takeout, because she forgot to eat again.

At **6:14 PM** the Engine breaches. June shoves him clear and seals the containment herself. She is gone before he stops sliding.

The prototype wrist unit — her backup, the **Second Hand** — is lying in the wreckage, still warm, still charged. He is not trained. He does not calibrate. He just *jumps* — and an uncalibrated jump falls all the way down her ladder, to the deepest anchor she ever pinged: **the Cretaceous.**

The only way back is the way she built: climb the anchors, era by era, to the lab, to 6:14 — and stop it.

He fails. He jumps again. He fails. He jumps again.

**The run structure is the story.** Every run ends at the accident. Every failure is a loop. And every loop he takes drags debris through history — which is why the eras are *bleeding into each other*.

### Why the moveset is canon
- **Dash** = the **Blink**: the Second Hand skips him between moments. His i-frames are literal — for a fraction of a second, he isn't *in* this second.
- **Perfect dodge** = the **Split Second**: a blink timed so perfectly he steps between two instants and steals the difference (charge refund, diegetic). The counter-strike it grants comes *from between seconds* — the one angle hardened time can't defend.
- **Melee monk kit** = he was already a fighter. The Engine gave him time; the dojo gave him everything else. **[YOUR CALL]:** name his discipline (one line of flavor, big charm return).
- **No healing** = his body has no present tense anymore. Time can't knit what isn't anchored. Wounds carry until the loop resets.

### Amber: the stagger system as lore
When time hardens — around fixed events, around beings history refuses to release — it sets like **amber**: translucent, gold, immovable. (Yes: the dinosaur biome hands you the metaphor in the first hour.)
- **Staggerable** = ordinary beings, loose in time.
- **Armored** = amber-crusted: souls and creatures fused to a fixed point. Ordinary hits skid off what time has decided. Only the **Split Second** cracks amber.
- **Immune** = **Menders**: the Custodian's paradox-antibodies, constructs *made* of set time (see §3). Can't be staggered, only killed.

Reserved palette slot: amber-gold is the "armored" gameplay color, everywhere, no exceptions.

## 2. Contamination: the art direction

Each loop bleeds history. The biomes are not clean eras — they are eras **infected by your attempts**: Greek bronze embedded in Cretaceous mud, a T-rex with spears healed into its hide, knights wielding scavenged future-tech, hieroglyphs describing "the man who falls from tomorrow."

This is the game's visual identity and its biggest solo-dev gift (see §7): mixing wildly different asset styles is normally incoherent — here it's *the point*, unified by one toon shader and one palette. Contamination can also *escalate across meta-progression*: the deeper your loop count, the more debris in the world. The world itself keeps score of your failures.

## 3. The Villain: the Custodian

Not future-you (he shows up — see Biome 5 — but he's not the end).

The **Custodian** is the reason you fail. A courteous, impeccably composed figure who exists at every moment simultaneously and experiences none of them. He carries a pocket watch that doesn't tick — it *points*, always, at 6:14. He is the immune system of causality: where time tears, he welds. And he has welded the accident shut.

Because here is the wound at the center of the game: **June's death is a containment weld.** The breach at 6:14 would have cracked time open — the Engine's failure cascading down every anchor she ever pinged, unstitching history from the Cretaceous up. Her sacrifice sealed it. The Custodian set that second in amber *because it is holding the world closed.* Save her, and everything tears.

So he is not hunting Cole out of malice. He meets him, run after run, at doors and crossroads, checks the watch that doesn't tick, and says some version of: *"She dies at 6:14. I am sorry. I am always sorry."* He is patient because he has nothing but time, and gentle because — **the reveal** — *he was a looper once.* Someone he could not save; thousands of attempts; and at the end of them, instead of breaking time, he became the thing that keeps it. He is not your opposite. He is your worst-case ending, promoted.

That's what makes him work as a repeat final boss where future-you wouldn't: shock twists spend themselves in one playthrough, but *a sympathetic jailer who might be right* generates new dialogue, new doubt, and new dramatic beats every single run — Hades-the-character's actual job in Hades.

**The true ending** (post-win epilogue arc, Hades-style): beating the Custodian doesn't save June — it proves you *could* tear the weld, which forces the real endgame: finding the third way. Repair the breach *and* pull her out — with the Deep Ages' help (§5), or by taking the Custodian's watch, or by June herself solving it from inside the frozen second she's sealed in. **[YOUR CALL]** — this is the ending you should author personally; three doors are open.

## 4. Biomes, Bosses, Sub-bosses

Structure mirrors Hades exactly: **four full biomes** (6–7 rooms each) **+ one short final gauntlet** (Styx-analog). Chronological ascent fixed to actual history: dinos → Egypt → Greece → medieval → present.

### Biome 1 — The Deep Anchor (Cretaceous)
Jungle, ash-sky, the impact comet visible and *paused* mid-fall — the anchor point itself, hanging overhead the whole biome. Contamination: bronze wreckage, torn banners, a crashed something from a later era half-swallowed by mud.
- **Sub-boss: the First Camper.** A time-tourist from a future that no longer exists, stranded decades ago (his subjective time), gone feral, armored in scavenged everything. Comic-tragic; may become a hub vendor after enough defeats. **[YOUR CALL]** on his redemption.
- **Boss: TYRANT.** The T-rex — but *yours*. It has died to you before. Every loop it comes back with the debris of your fights healed into it: spears in the hide, a bronze pauldron grown into shoulder, amber plating spreading (armored-tier tutorial made flesh). Meta-escalation candidate: Tyrant visually accumulates your loop count. The game's mascot monster — capsule-art material.

### Biome 2 — The Sand Anchor (New Kingdom Egypt)
Sun-hammered monument fields; the anchor is a foundation stone at a half-built Giza. Contamination: your loops are *in the walls* — hieroglyph friezes depicting the falling man, the blink, the woman in the sealed second. The court found your debris and made it holy.
- **Sub-boss: the Sphinx Engine.** A temple guardian rebuilt by court engineers around a future power core they excavated from your wreckage. Riddle-flavored telegraph fight: it announces attacks in advance and punishes you anyway.
- **Boss: the Twice-Crowned.** A fictional pharaoh who noticed the loops. He has seen you die in his court a hundred times and had every one recorded; he believes consuming your "eternity" will make his own. Fights with pre-knowledge of your habits (adaptive telegraph gimmick — reads your most-used strings). Historical-fiction, no real deities: state religion appears only as invented court ritual around *him*.

### Biome 3 — The Bright Anchor (Classical Greece)
Delphi under a paused eclipse — the anchor. Marble, cypress, long shadows at permanent dusk. The knowing wink at Hades lives here; keep it a wink, not a costume.
- **Sub-boss: the Oracle Who Remembers.** She sees every loop — past and future ones. Fight built on prophecy: she calls your moves before you make them; victory means deliberately fighting *badly*, off-rhythm (a skill-expression subversion). Afterward: a hub-visitable NPC and the story's exposition valve — the only being who can tell Cole what the Custodian is.
- **Boss: TALOS.** The bronze colossus of legend — animated not by ichor but by a reactor from a torn-open future, lodged where a heart should be. Pure armored-tier showcase: a boss that is *mostly* amber and bronze, cracked open only by Split Seconds. Public-domain famous, mechanically perfect for the stagger system.

### Biome 4 — The Iron Anchor (Medieval)
A comet-lit permanent night over a war camp that has forgotten what the war was for. Contamination peak: knights with jet-lances, siege engines with hydraulics, gunpowder that shouldn't exist yet.
- **Sub-boss: the Comet Court.** A trio of zealot knights who worship the anchor-comet (mini Theseus-energy pomp; rotating aggro puzzle).
- **Boss: MORDRED, the Knight Who Remembers.** The famous name you wanted — Arthurian, public domain, instantly legible as *the betrayed blade*. You killed him in an early loop; the death knocked him loose from time, and now he remembers **every fight**. He adapts across runs (scripted evolution tiers: new weapons scavenged from contaminated eras, counters to strategies the game has seen you use), and he *hates* you — not for killing him, but for making him like you: unstuck, unforgetting, unable to end. Your dark mirror one step early, and the recurring rival slot (Theseus/[Redacted] role) with real teeth. His arc can resolve — ally, mercy, or tragedy. **[YOUR CALL]**, and it's a good one.

### Biome 5 — 6:14 (final gauntlet, short)
Not "the future" — **the present, detonated.** The accident-second exploded into a paradox storm: the lab shattered across a kilometer of frozen time, future-tech debris raining upward, June visible in the amber weld at the center like a figure in a paperweight. Your "modern but futuristic" instinct, fully explained — every sci-fi asset here is wreckage from futures that keep failing to happen.
- **Mid-boss: the Veteran.** Future-you — exactly where you wanted him: *a* villain, not *the* villain. A Cole from thousands of loops deeper who broke, took the Custodian's side, and now guards the storm's edge to spare you what he became. He fights with your full kit plus everything you haven't learned yet. Beating him is the game's skill-check thesis: you are the version that doesn't give up.
- **Final boss: the CUSTODIAN, at the weld.** Phase 1: the immaculate gentleman, Menders orbiting, the watch pointing. Phase 2: the welds tear and you see what he's holding back — he fights while *keeping time shut*, one hand always on the wound. Phase 3 **[YOUR CALL]**: does June, inside the amber, intervene?

## 5. Boons: the Deep Ages

The six elements are not gods — they are **epochs of the deep past**, so old that history's bleeding doesn't touch them, reached through June's deepest calibration pings. They lend Cole their weight because the breach threatens even them — and because, unlike the Custodian, they remember what it was to *change*.

| Patron | Element (DamageType) | Sketch |
|---|---|---|
| **The Hadean** | Fire | The molten first Earth. Speaks in eruption metaphors; finds your species adorable and flammable. |
| **The Long Winter** | Ice | The glacial ages. Patient, vast, speaks rarely; boons arrive like a temperature drop. |
| **The Green Reach** | Nature | The Carboniferous. Cheerfully hungry; life as the thing that overgrows everything, including time. |
| **The First Breath** | Wind | The young atmosphere. Restless, quick, patron of the Blink; delighted by your dashes. |
| **The Bedrock** | Earth | The continental deep. Slow, load-bearing, disapproves of hurry, blesses you anyway. |
| **The Unspent** | Force | The first instant — the ignition of everything. Barely coherent; pure held-breath pressure released. |

Names are placeholders — **[YOUR CALL]** naming pass. Deep-Age interaction pairs (Fire+Wind, Ice+Bedrock…) slot into your pre-planned hit-modifier pipeline for future duo-boons. DamageType enum untouched.

**Other door rewards** (all diegetic): **wreckage** (meta-currency — literally the debris of your own loops), **June's notes** (scattered pages of her calibration journal = upgrades *and* her voice threaded through every biome — the emotional drip-feed), **anchor shards** (keepsake slots), **stranded souls** (unstuck beings you can send home — small permanent world changes), **Camper contracts** (challenge rooms).

## 6. The Hub: T-Minus One Hour

Every run begins in the same place: **the lab and the little apartment above it, one hour before the test.** The Second Hand holds this hour open — a bubble of borrowed time that resets when he jumps.

And **June is there.** Alive, warm, chalk on her sleeve, forgetting to eat. You talk to her before *every run*. She doesn't know. This is the emotional engine of the entire game and the thing Hades never had: the person you're fighting for is your hub, and every conversation is one hour before her death.

**Meta-progression of noticing:** as loop count climbs, she starts to catch it — *"…didn't you say that yesterday? No — never mind."* → déjà vu → dreams of amber → until, deep in the campaign, she quietly solves it from inside: she's the physicist; she figures out what's happening before you tell her. The endgame's third way (§3) may route through her. **[YOUR CALL]** how much she learns and when — author this arc yourself; it's the heart.

Other residents of the borrowed hour:
- **PETRI** — the lab's assistant AI, dragged half out of time with you; the only one who remembers every loop from the start. Codex, shop, run stats, dry commentary. (Chronicler/Hypnos energy.)
- **The cat.** The lab cat got unstuck too. Sells nothing. Remembers everything. Judges you. (Dusa-slot charm, zero dialogue budget.)
- **Unlockable visitors:** the Oracle (post-Biome-3), the First Camper (redeemed), Deep Ages via the Engine's receiver in the garden, and — late, rarely, unsettlingly — **the Custodian**, who sometimes takes tea in your kitchen at T-minus-fifty-nine, because he is everywhere and always, and he *likes* you.

## 7. Title

**Primary: BETWEEN SECONDS.** It names the dash, the Split Second, where Cole lives, and where June is sealed — the whole game in two words — and it's clean at capsule size. Pairs with the tagline: *"She dies at 6:14. You have all the time in the world."*

Backups: **CHRONOFIST** (dumber, punchier, extremely Steam — genuinely viable if you want action-first branding), **T-MINUS** (tense, short, but vague), **6:14** (art-house cool, terrible searchability — save it as the chapter title of the final biome instead). Avoid *Split Second* as a title (2010 Disney game, trademark risk).

Before committing: Steam search, USPTO/EUIPO, domains, socials.

## 8. Solo-Dev Asset Strategy (updated for this theme)

Governing rule unchanged: **coherence beats fidelity** — one master toon shader + one palette makes cheap assets read as intentional style.

**The contamination dividend (new, and big):** normally a solo dev mixing a dinosaur pack, an Egypt pack, a knight pack, and sci-fi props gets an incoherent mess. In this game, era-mixing is *canon*. You can kitbash across wildly different asset packs **as a feature**, unify them with the toon shader + palette strip, and the result reads as art direction, not budget. No other theme you could pick converts asset-store pragmatism into identity like this.

- **Master cel shader** (URP Shader Graph; Toony Colors Pro to skip a month): ramp + outline + rim reserved for gameplay info. **Amber-gold = armored**, everywhere. Amber itself is a shader showcase: translucent, refractive, glowing — one great amber shader carries the whole stagger-visual language.
- **Palette-strip texturing:** one gradient atlas, all meshes UV to palette bands. Per-biome palettes = one texture each; contamination props deliberately *keep their source-era palette band* so they pop as intrusions. Cheap, coherent, systematic.
- **Characters:** shared humanoid skeleton for all human-type enemies across eras (hoplite/guard/knight = same rig, different silhouettes + palettes). Tyrant and Talos are the two bespoke rigs worth real time — they're also your marketing images. Hub NPCs: **2D painted portraits + simple in-world models** (Hades' own trick); June's portrait set is the single highest-value art purchase/commission in the project.
- **Animation:** hand-keyframe the combat set (combos, Blink, Split Second, boss telegraphs) — game-feel is the product; Mixamo/packs for locomotion filler. Snappy low-frame strong-pose animation suits toon style and is faster to make.
- **VFX:** one stylized pack recolored to palette; hand-build the gameplay-critical set: telegraphs, Blink trail (chromatic "time-smear"), Split Second flash (screen-space time-freeze frame — cheap, iconic), amber cracks, per-element Deep Age effects.
- **SFX:** Sonniss GDC bundles + layering; record cloth-whoosh foley for the monk kit (one afternoon, huge ROI). Signature sound to design early: the **tick** — the Custodian's watch, the Split Second's beat. A game about time should have one perfect tick.
- **Music:** commission 3–5 anchor tracks (main theme, hub/June's theme, Mordred, Custodian, credits); the strong concept: **each biome's track is the same leitmotif arranged in that era's instrumentation** — June's theme on bone flute, on lyre, on hurdy-gurdy, on synths. Emotionally devastating, and it makes a small commission budget sound like a big score. License filler for the rest.
- **AI disclosure:** image-gen for moodboards you don't ship = free exploration. Anything AI-generated that ships goes in Steam's mandatory disclosure — decide per category up front. Claude-written code is standard practice and uncontroversial; shipped gen-art/music is where communities push back.
- **Order of operations:** shader + palette strip → Cole + full combat animset (vertical slice = game-feel) → Biome 1 enemy set + tileset + Tyrant → hub + June portraits → breadth.
